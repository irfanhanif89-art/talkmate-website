# The frameworks worth stealing — who they're from, and what to actually do

Researched 2026-08. This is the strategy layer behind `foodfomo-ugc-ads`. Read it before
planning a round. Every framework below is credited to the practitioner it came from, with
the FoodFOMO translation attached — the translation is ours, the framework is theirs.

---

## 1. Dara Denney — the five dimensions, and why persona is the one that matters

**Who:** performance-creative consultant; formerly senior director of performance marketing at
Thesis, with **$100M+ of ad spend** behind her. Widely treated as the most trusted single voice
on performance creative.

**The framework:** break every creative into five dimensions —
**Format · Creator · Messaging · Imagery · Persona.**

**Her 2026 argument:** the first four all feed into **persona**, because persona is the
dimension Meta's algorithm is actually grouping against. You are not testing videos. You are
testing *who this speaks to and what they already believe*.

**FoodFOMO translation.** Before writing a hook, name the person:
- *The reel-saver* — saves food videos constantly, visits almost none of them.
- *The new-in-town* — moved cities, doesn't know where to eat, defaults to chains.
- *The weekend planner* — the one who has to decide where the group eats on Saturday.
- *The night-out crowd* — 18+, drinks-led, wants the bar everyone's posting about.
- *The specific-need diner* — halal, or a cuisine craving they can't place near them.

Two ads aimed at the reel-saver are **one test**, however different they look.

## 2. Savannah Sanchez — native production, and the first three seconds

**Who:** The Social Savannah. Ran creative for top ecommerce brands across Facebook, TikTok,
Snapchat, Google and Pinterest; the most-cited practitioner on TikTok ad production
specifically.

**What she teaches:** ads that look like posts beat ads that look like ads. Shot-on-phone
framing, native audio, real speech, a hook that lands in the first three seconds. Polished,
brand-heavy production reliably *under*performs creator-style content on TikTok.

**FoodFOMO translation.** The ad should look like a Melbourne twenty-something filming in
their car outside a burger place — not like an app promo. Hand-held framing. Ambient noise.
No logo in the first frame. No music-video colour grade. If a frame looks like it came from a
design tool, it is wrong.

## 3. Barry Hott — hook-first, iterate the winner

**Who:** creative strategist, "Building Ads with Barry"; teaches hands-on ad building with
guest sessions from Denney.

**What to take:** the hook is a *separable component*. Build ads so the first three seconds
can be swapped without rebuilding the whole video — then iterate hooks on concepts that have
already proven they can hold attention.

**FoodFOMO translation.** Produce every concept with the hook as its own shot, so a losing
hook on a winning concept costs one re-generation, not a whole video. This is a production
instruction, not just a strategy one — it shapes how the Higgsfield shot list is built.

## 4. Motion's creative-testing discipline — concepts vs variations

**Who:** Motion, the creative analytics platform; its library aggregates Denney, Sanchez, Hott
and others. Their creative-testing guides are the standard reference.

**The framework:**
- A **concept** is the big idea. A **variation** is a re-execution of it.
- Pick **3–5 concepts per round**. Give each **one** simple variation to start.
- **Test multiple concepts, not multiple edits of one concept.** Testing only hook variations
  on a losing concept optimises a losing idea.
- Use **one consistent structure across Meta and TikTok** so results are comparable.

**FoodFOMO translation.** Round 1 is five concepts × one variation = five ads. Not one concept
with five hooks. That comes in round 2, on whichever concept survives.

## 5. The diagnostic metrics — hook rate and hold rate

The metrics that tell you *which part* of an ad is broken. CTR alone can't.

| Metric | Formula | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook rate** | 3-second views ÷ impressions | TikTok: **30%+ healthy, 40%+ elite.** Meta: **25% table stakes, 30%+ best-in-class.** Under 30% = the hook needs work. |
| **Hold rate** | ThruPlays ÷ 3-second views | **40–50% typical.** |

Read them as a matrix, in this order — hook (0–3s), then hold (3–15s), then CTR, then
conversion. Each stage only matters if the one before it passed.

**The trap to avoid:** a beautiful ad with a 12% hook rate is not "a bit underperforming",
it is invisible. Nine out of ten people never saw the part you agonised over.

## 6. Mobile app UA — the constraint layer

Sources: Business of Apps, adjoe, RevenueCat, Admiral Media, TikTok Ads Manager (2026 guides).

**What's true for apps specifically:**
- Meta and TikTok are both viable for early app growth. **TikTok App Promotion campaigns now
  perform competitively with Meta** for consumer apps.
- **Spark Ads** turn real creator posts into paid ads with native trust signals — the highest-
  performing TikTok app format when a real creator is available.
- TikTok rewards accounts that treat the channel as a **creative testing engine**, not a media
  buy. Volume and iteration beat a single perfect asset.
- **AI-generated UGC-style video is now an established, effective format** — scalable
  production with the native feel the audience expects. This is precisely what Higgsfield gives
  FoodFOMO.
- Real app campaigns need a **calibrated MMP** (AppsFlyer, Adjust, Singular) plus SKAN 4.
  **FoodFOMO has none of this.** Until it does, install-optimised campaigns are off the table
  and traffic-to-App-Store-link is the honest play. See `measurement.md`.

## 7. What we deliberately did *not* copy

- **Aggressive scarcity and false urgency.** Standard in DTC, structurally wrong for a product
  whose differentiator is telling you when the hype is fake.
- **Founder-face ads.** They work, but they need a founder who wants to be on camera weekly.
  Revisit only if Irfan or Jade opts in.
- **Discount-led offers.** FoodFOMO is free. There is nothing to discount; the offer is the
  food, and the promise is that it's worth the trip.
