# FoodFOMO concept bank — round 1

Five concepts, one per persona, deliberately different *ideas* rather than five outfits on one
idea. Each carries a falsifier: the result that would prove the concept wrong. Scripts are
starting points to be read aloud and rewritten in the speaker's own rhythm, never recited.

**Geo for round 1: Melbourne and Sydney only** (68 and 62 live spots). Not Canberra, not Hobart.

---

## C1 — "The graveyard" · persona: the reel-saver

**The insight.** Everyone has hundreds of saved food videos and has visited approximately none
of them. The saved folder is where good intentions go to die. This is the sharpest pain
FoodFOMO actually solves, and nobody else names it out loud.

**Hook options (first 3s, spoken):**
1. "I've got 400 food videos saved and I've been to four of them."
2. "Show me your saved folder and I'll tell you what you had for dinner. It was Uber Eats."
3. "This is the saddest folder on my phone."

**Beats (15s):** hook over a scroll of a saved folder → "the problem isn't the videos, it's
that you never find out *where* it was" → app: same video, venue attached, directions →
"it's the saved folder, except you can actually go" → CTA.

**Workflow:** `ugc-saas-flow` (the app has to be on screen — the payoff is visual).
**Falsifier:** strong hook rate, weak hold → the pain lands but the solution doesn't read.

---

## C2 — "Overrated or worth it" · persona: the weekend planner

**The insight.** The brand's honesty spine, weaponised. Every food app tells you what's good.
This one tells you what's overhyped. That is a genuinely novel promise and it earns attention
from people who are tired of being sold to.

**Hook options:**
1. "This place has 2 million views. It's not worth the queue."
2. "An app that tells you when viral food is actually rubbish? Finally."
3. "Everyone's queuing for this. I'm about to tell you not to."

**Beats:** hook on a hyped dish → "everyone films it, nobody tells you if it's worth 40
minutes in a line" → app: people who went, rating hype vs reality → "so you know before you
drive" → CTA.

**Workflow:** `ugc-flow` with app cutaways, or `ugc-saas-flow`.
**Falsifier:** low CTR despite good hold → people enjoy the take but don't want an app for it.
**Watch:** never name a real venue as "rubbish" in a paid ad. Keep the negative example
generic or unidentifiable — see the guardrails at the bottom.

## C3 — "New here" · persona: the new-in-town

**The insight.** Moving cities is when food habits are genuinely up for grabs. High intent,
clear moment, and Melbourne/Sydney both have constant inbound.

**Hook options:**
1. "Moved to Melbourne three weeks ago and I've eaten at the same place five times."
2. "If you've just moved here, don't ask Google where to eat."
3. "Nobody tells you the hardest part of moving cities is dinner."

**Beats:** hook → "Google gives you the places that paid for it, TikTok gives you videos with
no address" → app: city picker, feed fills with real local spots → one specific venue named →
CTA.

**Workflow:** `ugc-flow`.
**Falsifier:** works in Melbourne, dies in Sydney → it's a city-fit story, not a concept.

---

## C4 — "The 8pm argument" · persona: the group decider

**The insight.** "Where do you want to eat?" "I don't mind." The most universally recognised
food conversation there is, and a moment of genuine decision paralysis.

**Hook options:**
1. "'Where do you want to eat?' 'I don't mind.' 'Okay, where?' 'I don't mind.'"
2. "We spent 25 minutes deciding and ended up at the same place again."
3. "The couple's argument that isn't actually about food."

**Beats:** re-enacted exchange → app opened, feed scrolled, one card autoplays → "decided in
about nine seconds" → CTA.

**Workflow:** `ugc-flow` (performance-led; the comedy carries it) with a screen cutaway.
**Falsifier:** high hook, high hold, no installs → entertaining, not persuasive. The ad became
the product.

---

## C5 — "Food-first" · persona: broad / the hungry scroll

**The insight.** The control. No creator, no story — just the food, hard cuts, a voiceover, and
the app as the answer. Every round needs one concept that competes on appetite alone, because
sometimes the food *is* the ad.

**Hook options:**
1. "Melbourne, this is what you're missing." (over a cheese pull)
2. "Six places in Melbourne worth cancelling plans for."
3. No spoken hook — food, sound up, text on screen: "all of this is 20 minutes from you."

**Beats:** rapid food cuts → "all of these are in the app, with directions" → CTA.

**Workflow:** `ugc-product-flow`.
**Falsifier:** high hook, low CTR → appetite gets attention but doesn't create app intent.
**Watch:** the food must be **generated**, not scraped from the feed. See guardrails.

## Script rules

1. **Read it aloud.** If you wouldn't say it to a mate, rewrite it.
2. **One idea per ad.** An ad that says three things says nothing.
3. **Name something real** — a suburb, a dish, a queue. Specificity is the whole trick.
4. **The CTA is a sentence, not a slogan:** "It's free, it's called FoodFOMO, it's on the App
   Store." Never "download now to unlock your foodie journey".
5. **Australian.** Written and spoken. A US voice in an AU-only campaign is a tell.
6. **Never over-hype.** The product tells you when hype is fake; the ads can't be the thing it
   warns you about.

## Guardrails

- **No scraped creator videos.** Every frame is generated or shot by us.
- **No real venue named negatively.** Positive mentions of real venues are fine as places that
  appear in the app; never imply partnership or endorsement.
- **No synthetic real people.** The creator persona is fictional, or it's Irfan/Jade with
  explicit consent via `higgsfield-soul-id`.
- **AI disclosure label on every ad.** Mandatory on TikTok since 2026-07-21.
- **18+ targeting**, matching the App Store rating.
